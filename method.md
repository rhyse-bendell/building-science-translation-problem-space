# Method

## What is Group Concept Mapping?

**Group Concept Mapping (GCM)** is a structured method for eliciting, organizing, rating, and interpreting stakeholder perspectives on a complex issue. Participants generate statements in response to a shared prompt, sort those statements into meaningful groups, rate them on defined dimensions, and help interpret the resulting maps and themes.

GCM combines qualitative input with quantitative analysis while preserving a clear connection to participants' original ideas. The result is a visual and structured representation of how a group understands a problem space.

## Why GCM fits this project

Science translation involves multiple communities, settings, stages, and definitions of success. GCM can bring those perspectives together without requiring the research team to impose a complete structure in advance. It makes areas of convergence and difference visible and supports discussion of relationships, priorities, and potential leverage points.

## How participant input becomes project data

Participant statements form the initial data set. After the research team reviews and synthesizes them into a clear, manageable statement set, participants organize the statements by conceptual similarity and rate them on project-relevant dimensions. Statistical analysis of those responses produces maps and patterns for interpretation. Participants may then help name clusters, explain findings, identify gaps, and consider practical implications.

Individual contributions will be combined into project-level results. Activity-specific instructions will explain what input is requested at each stage.
