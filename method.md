  <h1>Group Concept Mapping</h1>
  <p class="lead">A structured method for eliciting, organizing, rating, and interpreting stakeholder perspectives on a complex problem.</p>
</header>

<section class="content-section" aria-labelledby="gcm-does-heading">
  <div class="two-column two-column-wide">
    <div><p class="section-label">The method</p><h2 id="gcm-does-heading">What Group Concept Mapping does</h2></div>
    <div class="prose-block"><p>Group Concept Mapping (GCM) combines qualitative stakeholder input with structured sorting and rating activities and quantitative mapping. Participants generate statements about a complex problem, organize those statements by perceived similarity, and rate them on key dimensions.</p><p>The research team analyzes the sorting and rating data to produce maps, themes, and other interpretable patterns. Participants and project contributors then help interpret what those patterns mean.</p></div>
  </div>

    <img src="{{ '/assets/img/gcm-process.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Infographic showing Group Concept Mapping moving from data collection, to analysis, to results including point maps, cluster maps, pattern matches, and go-zones.">
    <figcaption>Group Concept Mapping translates stakeholder input into structured maps, rating summaries, and interpretable outputs that support planning and decision-making.</figcaption>
  </figure>
</section>

<section class="section-band" aria-labelledby="project-use-heading">
  <div class="section-inner two-column">
    <div><p class="section-label">Project application</p><h2 id="project-use-heading">How GCM is used in this project</h2></div>
    <div class="prose-block"><p>GCM creates a traceable path from stakeholder experience to a structured problem space for science translation. It allows perspectives from defense, industry, academia, and adjacent communities to be collected broadly, organized systematically, and kept visible in the developing framework.</p><p>Activity-specific instructions will explain the input requested at each stage. The resulting maps and summaries will be interpreted with project contributors rather than treated as findings in isolation.</p></div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="content-section" aria-labelledby="method-process-heading">
  <p class="section-label">Six stages</p><h2 id="method-process-heading">Process at a glance</h2>
  <ol class="process-band process-band-detailed">
    <li><strong>Planning and framing</strong><span>Define the scope and prepare project activities.</span></li>
    <li><strong>Idea generation</strong><span>Gather perspectives in response to a project prompt.</span></li>
    <li><strong>Statement synthesis</strong><span>Clean and synthesize responses into a focused set.</span></li>
    <li><strong>Sorting and rating</strong><span>Group statements by similarity and rate key dimensions.</span></li>
    <li><strong>Analysis and mapping</strong><span>Create maps and summaries from the structured data.</span></li>
    <li><strong>Interpretation and use</strong><span>Interpret patterns and consider their planning value.</span></li>
  </ol>
  <aside class="method-note" aria-label="From input to output"><strong>From input to output</strong><span>Stakeholder input <b aria-hidden="true">→</b> statements <b aria-hidden="true">→</b> sorting and rating data <b aria-hidden="true">→</b> maps and themes <b aria-hidden="true">→</b> interpreted framework</span></aside>
</section>

<section class="section-band" aria-labelledby="outputs-heading">
  <div class="section-inner">
    <p class="section-label">Method products</p><h2 id="outputs-heading">Typical outputs</h2>
    <p class="section-intro"><strong>These are typical or expected outputs of the method, not current project findings.</strong></p>
    <div class="card-grid method-output-grid">
      <article class="card output-card"><h3>Point map</h3><p>Positions statements in relation to one another based on how participants sorted them by similarity.</p></article>
      <article class="card output-card"><h3>Cluster map</h3><p>Organizes related statements into broader themes that can be named and interpreted.</p></article>
      <article class="card output-card"><h3>Rating summaries</h3><p>Summarize how statements or clusters are rated on the dimensions used in the project.</p></article>
      <article class="card output-card"><h3>Pattern comparisons</h3><p>Show where stakeholder groups or rating dimensions converge or differ.</p></article>
      <article class="card output-card"><h3>Priority areas and leverage points</h3><p>Highlight issues stakeholders view as important, feasible, actionable, or useful for decisions.</p></article>
      <article class="card output-card"><h3>Stakeholder-informed framework</h3><p>Integrates interpreted results into a structured account of the science translation problem space.</p></article>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="content-section" aria-labelledby="interpretation-heading">
  <div class="two-column two-column-wide">
    <div><p class="section-label">From maps to action</p><h2 id="interpretation-heading">How results support interpretation and planning</h2></div>
    <div class="prose-block"><p>Maps and ratings provide a shared structure for discussing a complex system. They can help contributors examine relationships among issues, compare perspectives, identify coordination needs, and distinguish broad concerns from more focused opportunities for action.</p><p>Collaborative interpretation adds context to the visual and quantitative outputs. Together, these elements can inform future research, program planning, stakeholder coordination, and practical improvements in how research findings are moved toward use.</p></div>
  </div>
</section>
