# Group Concept Mapping

Group Concept Mapping (GCM) is this project's primary method for turning stakeholder perspectives into structured, interpretable outputs. It provides a systematic way to elicit, organize, rate, and interpret perspectives on the complex problem of science translation.

## What Group Concept Mapping does

GCM combines qualitative stakeholder input with structured sorting and rating activities and quantitative mapping. Participants generate statements about a complex problem, organize those statements by similarity, and rate them on key dimensions. The research team then analyzes the sorting and rating data to produce maps, themes, and other interpretable patterns.

Participants and other project contributors also help interpret the resulting maps and themes. This combination of stakeholder input, structured analysis, and collaborative interpretation supports the development of a structured problem space for science translation.

## How GCM is used in this project

The project follows a sequence designed to preserve the connection between stakeholder perspectives and the resulting framework:

1. Participants respond to a project prompt.
2. The research team cleans and synthesizes the responses into a set of statements.
3. Participants sort the statements into groups based on their perceived similarity.
4. Participants rate the statements on project-relevant dimensions.
5. The research team analyzes the sorting and rating data to produce visual maps and rating summaries.
6. Participants and project contributors help interpret what the maps and themes mean for science translation.

Activity-specific instructions will explain what input is requested during each project stage.

## Why this method fits the project

Science translation involves multiple communities, roles, pathways, and constraints. GCM is useful for this project because it allows perspectives to be collected broadly, organized systematically, and translated into maps and themes. Those outputs can support collaborative interpretation, prioritization, and planning while keeping stakeholder input visible in the developing account of the problem space.
