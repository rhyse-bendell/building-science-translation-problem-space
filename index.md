This project examines how scientific findings move from research settings into real-world use. It is building a structured problem space by identifying the actors, contexts, barriers, facilitators, leverage points, and coordination needs that shape whether research becomes usable policy, programs, technologies, practices, or capabilities.
{: .lead }

## Why this project exists

Science translation is rarely a single, linear handoff. Research can move among funders, researchers, developers, decision-makers, practitioners, and intended users, each working with different priorities, evidence needs, timelines, and operating conditions. A shared view of this system can make persistent gaps and dependencies visible and help stakeholders identify where coordinated attention may improve the path from research to use.

## How the project works

The project uses **Group Concept Mapping**, a structured participatory method. Stakeholders generate statements, organize them into meaningful groups, rate them on key dimensions, and help interpret the resulting maps and themes. [Explore the method]({{ '/method/' | relative_url }}).
