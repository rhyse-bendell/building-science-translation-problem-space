# About the project

## Purpose

**Building a Problem Space for Science Translation** is an Army Research Office-funded project examining how scientific findings move from research settings into real-world use. The project will identify the actors, contexts, barriers, facilitators, and leverage points that shape whether research becomes usable policy, programs, technologies, practices, or capabilities.

## Why this project is needed

Science translation is rarely a simple or linear handoff. Research may pass among funders, researchers, developers, decision-makers, practitioners, and intended users, with feedback and adaptation needed along the way. Priorities, language, incentives, resources, evidence requirements, timelines, and operating conditions may differ across defense, academia, industry, and adjacent communities.

A shared view of this larger system can make persistent gaps and dependencies visible. By bringing multiple perspectives into one structured problem space, the project can identify where coordination breaks down, what supports progress, and which issues merit focused attention.

## What the project will produce

- **A science translation problem map** describing major barriers, facilitators, stakeholder roles, contextual factors, and coordination needs.
- **A stakeholder-informed framework** showing how translation processes vary across defense, academia, industry, and adjacent communities—and where coordination can improve.
- **Priority areas and leverage points** reflecting the problems, relationships, and intervention points stakeholders view as important, feasible, and actionable.
- **Briefings, reports, and scholarly products** supporting future research, program planning, stakeholder coordination, and practical improvements in moving research toward use.

## Project structure

The work is organized around three primary stakeholder pillars: defense, industry, and academia. An Advisory Group provides high-level guidance; a Core Group contributes in-depth discussion and structured sensemaking; and an Extended Group offers broader asynchronous input. The research team designs and manages the study, analyzes participant data, and prepares project outputs.

Learn more about the [participant groups]({{ '/groups/' | relative_url }}) and the [Group Concept Mapping method]({{ '/method/' | relative_url }}).

## How outputs will be used

Project outputs are intended to create a shared, evidence-informed foundation for discussing science translation. They may inform future research questions, program and engagement strategies, coordination across stakeholder communities, and practical efforts to strengthen the path from research to use. Public products will be posted to [Project materials]({{ '/materials/' | relative_url }}) after review and approval.
