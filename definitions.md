# Definitions

> These working definitions are provided to support shared understanding across stakeholder communities. They will be refined as the project develops.

They establish a common starting point rather than final analytic claims. Participant input may reveal important differences in how communities use these terms.

<dl class="glossary">
  <dt>Science translation</dt>
  <dd>The iterative process of turning scientific findings, technologies, methods, models, or evidence-based practices into practical, usable, scalable, and sustainable applications in operational, policy, organizational, or professional practice contexts.</dd>

  <dt>Transition</dt>
  <dd>The movement of a research result, technology, method, or practice toward a specific applied setting, responsible organization, user, program, or capability. Transition may be one part of the broader translation process.</dd>

  <dt>Defense-related research</dt>
  <dd>Scientific or technical inquiry with current or potential relevance to defense missions, organizations, personnel, operations, decisions, or capabilities.</dd>

  <dt>Operational use</dt>
  <dd>Application in the real-world settings, decisions, practices, systems, or missions where work is performed and outcomes are produced.</dd>

  <dt>Policy, program, technology, practice, or capability</dt>
  <dd>The range of forms an applied research contribution may take: guidance or decisions; organized initiatives; tools or systems; repeatable ways of working; or an ability to achieve a desired effect.</dd>

  <dt>Barrier</dt>
  <dd>A scientific, technical, organizational, financial, policy, cultural, workforce, or collaborative condition that impedes, delays, or prevents translation.</dd>

  <dt>Facilitator</dt>
  <dd>A condition, resource, relationship, or action that enables, supports, or accelerates translation.</dd>

  <dt>Leverage point</dt>
  <dd>A place within the translation system where a focused intervention may produce meaningful improvement in processes, relationships, or outcomes.</dd>

  <dt>Coordination need</dt>
  <dd>A requirement for communication, alignment, sequencing, role clarity, information exchange, or shared action among actors and organizations.</dd>

  <dt>Stakeholder group</dt>
  <dd>A community of people or organizations that participates in, influences, is affected by, or has relevant knowledge of the research-to-use pathway.</dd>
</dl>
